Abstract

This project asks whether historical search and engagement signals can rank content pages that are more likely to experience click improvement in the following month.

Using the FlyRank internship warehouse, the analysis constructs a client × content dataset from March 2026 historical signals and April 2026 click outcomes.

A transparent Week 4 baseline is compared with Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest models using a client-grouped train/test split.

On the held-out test set, the Random Forest achieved 41.93% Precision@10% and 2.00× lift, compared with 38.40% and 1.83× for the transparent baseline.

The resulting ranking is intended as decision support for prioritizing pages for human review, not as a causal prediction of the effect of refreshing a page.

1. Problem Statement

The decision supported by this project is which content pages an editor should review first for potential improvement.

The unit of analysis is client × content. The output is a ranked opportunity score for eligible content pages.

A human editor can use the ranking to prioritize pages for further investigation, including reviewing click capture, search-result alignment, and content relevance.

The cost of a wrong call is spending limited editorial time on a page that does not subsequently show click improvement.

The system is therefore designed as decision support, not an automatic refresh decision.

Target

The target is 1 when April average clicks are greater than March average clicks, and 0 otherwise.

Population decision

Rows without usable March avg_position were excluded because the Week 4 baseline depends on this signal. Position availability was strongly associated with the target, so these values were not blindly imputed.

Pseudonymous client and content IDs were used for grouping and joining only, never as predictive features.

5. Interpretation

The Random Forest placed the greatest feature importance on historical search visibility and click activity.

Feature Importance
avg_impressions 59.17%
avg_clicks 20.50%
avg_position 7.52%
avg_pageviews 6.42%

These importances describe the fitted model and should not be interpreted as causal effects.

Error analysis showed that some highly ranked pages did not subsequently improve. Pages with measurable search visibility and very low historical click activity can be difficult to distinguish from successful opportunities using the available features.

6. Limitations & Honest Framing

  • The target measures whether average clicks increased from one month to the next; it does not measure whether a page actually needed a content refresh.
  • The modeling population was restricted to rows with usable March average search position. This limits generalization to pages with usable Search Console position data.
  • The evaluation is grouped by client and therefore measures generalization to clients not used for training.
  • The Random Forest improvement over the baseline was measurable but modest: 41.93% versus 38.40% Precision@10%.
  • The ranking identifies directional opportunities rather than causal effects.
  • The ranked queue should be treated as decision support rather than an automatic content-refresh decision.

7. Ranked Recommendations

The Random Forest produces an opportunity score for eligible content pages. Higher scores receive higher review priority.

Recommended workflow

  1. Prioritize the highest-ranked pages from the Random Forest queue.
  2. Review click capture and content/search-result alignment for pages with measurable visibility and low historical click activity.
  3. Use the score to prioritize human investigation rather than automatically refreshing content.
  4. Compare editorial decisions and later outcomes against the ranked queue before treating the system as a production rule.
Recommendation: use the Random Forest as the preferred experimental ranking model, while retaining the transparent W04 baseline as a simple benchmark.

The highest-ranked examples are pseudonymous content identifiers only; no client-identifying information is exposed.
